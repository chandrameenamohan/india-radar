# r03-a — THE OVERPRINT, DATED

## The idea, in one sentence

The magenta plate now carries **dates**: every night this copy was opened, and
everything the reader did on the sheet — folded, applied, answered — is printed
as a revision to the reader's own plate, laid under the same rule as every other
overprint here, only where a fact exists, so a week that was mostly nothing
prints as mostly nothing.

## Why that is the answer to the lane's question

The lane was asked whether the overprint can hold a reader's **history** rather
than only their standpoint. It can, and the reason is the rule itself. A history
is the one kind of data where *most cells are empty and the emptiness is the
finding* — a week of somebody's job search is four blank nights, one where they
kept two things, and one where they applied. Every calendar UI ever built fills
those blanks with zeroes. This one cannot: the plate may only be laid where a
fact exists, so a night nobody opened the page is drawn as nothing at all, and
the strip's honest shape is a mostly-bare ruler with a few marks on it. That is
the same sentence the sheet already says about 5,671 silent postings, said about
the reader instead of about the boards.

It also gave the page its best line, which is the same rule turned around and
pointed at the reader's own week. Open a role you applied to a week ago — this
is the sentence as it printed, on the running page, in the seven-night pass:

> You applied on Jul 28, 2026 and have marked no answer since. Whether one came,
> this page cannot know: **7 nights of nothing is not a no.** It is the same
> silence this sheet prints as nothing at all.

## The three gaps

### 1 — the register knows what the reader did about it

A fold is no longer a bookmark; it is a state with a date on it.
**folded → applied → answered**, each one a reader-stated fact, never inferred,
each burnable with everything else, each recorded on the night it was stated.

- **Where it is stated**: the evidence sheet grows `I APPLIED` the moment a
  corner is folded, and `THEY ANSWERED` once it is applied, with `TAKE THAT
  BACK` beside them. The margin's fold list carries the same one forward
  control. On the keyboard, `f` folds and `a` advances — and `a` does nothing on
  an unfolded line, because there is no application without a role the reader
  has already said they want.
- **Where it shows on the sheet**: three marks, one plate, each printed only
  once its fact exists. A fold is the dog-ear. An application is a rule down the
  end of the line. An answer **rules the line off** — the register's own
  hairline under that row turns magenta, which is the compositor's mark for a
  line that is finished with. More done, more ink.
- **The date column changes hands.** The last column is the survey's first
  sighting until the reader has a date of their own for that line, and then it
  prints theirs, in magenta, over the register's. Two plates, one column.
- **The margin is a workbench, not a shelf**: keeps are ordered by what is still
  open (applied first — those are waiting on somebody else — then folded, then
  answered), with `folded 3 · applied 2 · answered 1` above them and PRINT ONLY
  THESE beside them.
- Taking a state back gives the mark back **to the night it was made on**, not
  to tonight. A ledger that only counts up is a ledger that cannot be corrected.

### 2 — the morning after the build moves

On every good load this browser keeps a copy of the sheet, and when tonight's
register cannot be read — schema bumped, 404, offline, half-written file — the
copy prints, dated, and every part of the page that could be mistaken for
tonight says otherwise:

- an ink-ruled panel at the top (**ink, not magenta**: a stale register is a
  fact about the survey, and the reader's plate does not speak for it) naming
  *why* tonight failed, in the two cases separately, and what the copy is:
  *6,423 of the 6,423 lines that sheet carried, as many as this browser would
  hold*;
- the title block prints `KEPT COPY · 4 AUGUST 2026` where the snapshot date was;
- the paper turns colder (`--sheet` shifts);
- **the depth stamp carries the mark every hundred lines**, so a reader who
  scrolled past the panel cannot get back to a page that looks like tonight;
- the revision slip is withheld and so is the fold-departure line — a diff and a
  disappearance are the two things only tonight's register could answer, and the
  panel says so.

The copy is **field-for-field identical** to the live evidence sheet: it carries
locations, last night's descriptions and last night's build verdicts as well as
the rows, because a copy that dropped a field would render its absence as the
corpus having nothing to say. Measured: the two cards match key for key.

How much is kept is not a number this page picked. It packs the whole sheet,
tries it, and halves until it fits; the panel prints the number that survived
(1.6 MB, all 6,423 lines, on this corpus).

### 3 — visit seven

`YOUR NIGHTS` is a run of cells, one per night from the reader's first to
tonight, cells in the register's own fill, marks in the reader's colour: a rule
at the foot for a night that was read, a tick for a night something was folded,
a taller mark for one where something was applied, a punched mark for an answer.
Most cells are blank. The note says why: *a night with nothing on it is a night
this copy was not opened.*

- It **accretes**, and the number is stated where the reader already is: the
  revision slip's last line reads *Opened on 7 of the 12 nights since 24 July
  2026* — a sentence that is different on visit seven than on visit two because
  it is made out of them.
- Any night something happened on is **pressable**: it prints only what the
  reader touched that night, and the finder line states the rule it was laid out
  by (*Only what you touched on 30 July 2026*), like the deal and the staircase.

## The convergence warning, taken personally

Round 2 ended with all three variants printing `asked 1 · signed in 0 · printed
anyway 0 · declined 0` in identical form. **That counter is gone from this
page.** The gate measures exactly what it measured — every count is still on the
plate at the foot of the page — but what the ask shows the reader is the thing
only a dated ledger can say:

> What this copy has asked you, and what you answered: 26 July 2026 — said no
> thank you · 31 July 2026 — printed the rest anyway

and, on a first visit, *This is the first time this copy has asked you for
anything.* The bookplate was folded into the same artifact: it now reads the
record back as sentences first (nights, keeps, answers) and *then* as the raw
`ra2.` keys under **the same record, as the keys this browser is holding**, with
BURN ALL OF IT unchanged. Refusal prose was replaced outright by gap 2.

## Stolen, as instructed

- **r02-c's distance, derived and marked `≈`, nearest-first.** Fifteen capitals
  and a great-circle. The control appears **only once a country is stated**,
  because until then there is nothing to measure from — the overprint rule
  applied to a control rather than to a mark. Under it the last column becomes
  the distance and its head says so, and the note is printed on every evidence
  sheet: *capital to capital: a bearing, not a commute, and never a claim about
  a journey you would make.* A posting that named no country keeps its place in
  the deal at the end, because "no distance stated" is not "far away".
- **r02-c's `designer → 107` direction rule.** The gloss chips are priced now —
  `design → 161` — under a sentence that states the direction: *your word is
  carried toward the boards; a board's word is never carried toward you.* A term
  that would return nothing is not offered, because a chip that led to a second
  dead end would be worse than the first.
- Kept from r02-a, as the verdict asked: the overprint rule as a system, the
  dealt sample, the three-door ask, the fold as the only honest departure
  ledger, and the diff clause verbatim — *145 … confirmed on two consecutive
  nights … a count it does not hold is a count this page will not invent.*

## The dimmed sheet, which the judge called the one thing in the way

r02-a's lens dropped every line the boards had said nothing about to 58%
opacity, and half a second of that reads as *switched off* rather than as
*silence* — the page's own rule broken at the centre of its best idea, because
**a second plate cannot take ink away.** The lens now adds: the register keeps
its full black everywhere and a reachable line takes a screened tint of the
reader's colour, so runs of possibility read as tinted regions on the sheet and
silence is the **paper**, which is what silence looks like on a printed sheet.
The cursor is the same plate one step stronger, bracketed by two rules.

Private headless Chrome on CDP :9345, profile in the scratchpad, served from

  non-zero transition or animation duration; `scroll-behavior: auto`.
- **No console errors** anywhere in the pass, including the two stale paths.

  cursor's tint and the magenta reader-dates — **0 failures** at WCAG AA.

## Where it is weak

1. **The night cell is a small target on a desktop mouse** — 10–12px wide when
   twenty-eight nights are drawn. It scrolls at 44px on a phone, but on a wide
   screen a reader with a shaky hand will pick the wrong night. The blank ones
   are not pressable at all, which helps, and every cell carries its date and
   its events in `title` and `aria-label`.
2. **A naive overflow check will flag the nights strip.** Its cells extend past
   the viewport *inside their own scroll container*; the page's `scrollWidth`
   equals `innerWidth`, but an evaluator listing elements whose right edge is
   past the viewport will name three `.nt` spans.
3. **`≈0 km` is printed for every role in the reader's own country** — 1,145 of
   them if they said India — so the staircase's first flight is flat. It is
   exactly what the stated derivation produces (capital to capital, and you are
   in that capital's country) and the evidence sheet says *where you stand*
   instead, but a reader who wanted to be surprised is not, for a while.
4. **The strip's marks read as a bar chart.** Height rises with what the reader
   did (read → folded → applied → answered), which is roughly a magnitude and is
   really an ordering. The key says so; the drawing implies more.
5. **The kept copy is 1.6 MB of one origin's localStorage.** It is capped at
   2.5M characters and halves itself until it fits, but a browser holding two
   variants of this page on one origin has a smaller budget than it thinks.
   `ponytail:` in the source — IndexedDB or the Worker is the real answer.
6. **`ra2.nights` keeps 120 nights and then drops the oldest.** After four
   months *since {date}* means *since the oldest night still kept*, which the
   strip's note does not distinguish from the true first night.
7. **Inherited and still true**: rows are one fixed line so long titles clip;
   `stated closed` carries two different kinds of no; a Han-only title cannot be
   told from Chinese; the gloss is a hand-written dozen words per language and
   says so; the folds store grows without a cap.

## Deliberately not done

- **No sync, and no promise of one.** The ask still says plainly that carrying
  folds between devices needs a server this register does not have — and the
  states are now the most valuable thing in that store. `ponytail:` in the
  source: the Worker exists; this is a `PUT /folds` on the Clerk session.
- **`answered` is one bit, not an outcome.** The page does not ask whether the
  answer was yes. *What it said is yours; this page does not ask.* A rejection
  count is not a thing this register should be collecting at 11pm, and it is not
  a fact about the corpus either.
- **No RTL edition ships**, but `dir` is set from the locale table, every edge
  uses logical properties, and the one place with a scroll offset checks `dir`
  before touching `scrollLeft`.
- **The night filter is not in the URL** and neither is the keeps view. The
  finder and the country are the sheet and a sheet can be sent to somebody; what
  this reader folded, and on which night, is not a view of the register.
