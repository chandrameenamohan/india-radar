so we can find out whether anybody actually wants an account, before putting a
public register behind one" — which is `HANDOFF.md`'s rationale, said to the
reader's face. It arrives after 240 real roles from 79 companies, the remainder
is stated in numbers, place/words/typed-text survive sign-in, declining prints

the sentence. The bookplate prints every byte including the sign-in provider's,
labelled as not ours, and: "What you said about yourself is deliberately not in
the address bar either: a link is a thing you send to somebody else."

One wrinkle keeps it at 23.5: "could carry them to your phone" is a conditional
about a sync that does not exist (the builder's own `ponytail:` says so). The
wording survives scrutiny — *could*, not *will* — but it is the closest any
variant comes to selling undelivered function, where A chose the scrupulous
version of the same truth.

## Originality — 18

A second draft that went deeper rather than shinier. The lane assignment —
per-locale clause ordering — is delivered and it is not cosmetic: Japanese
reads SOV with its particles ("十五か国すべてから、どの分野の、どんな言葉を含
む職を、新しい順に見せて。"), German declines its prepositions and its
subjunctive ("Ich bräuchte ein gesponsertes Visum"), and the typed search word
sits *inside* the sentence as a lamp-coloured object in every language. The
sentence now reaches the reader (second sentence), the keeps (a subject), the
diff (a clause — "first seen since …"), and the empty state. That is a metaphor
carried all the way through, which is what this axis rewards.

Also a decision, and the right one: when the corpus moved to schema v11 under
this session, B's own headline numbers became **em-dashes** — "— open roles at
— funded companies … on —" — with "fields may have moved" as the reason. The
refusal renders as absence in its own typography.

Not more than 18 because the frame itself is inherited from its own round-1
self — the invention quantum here is extension, executed superbly, of an idea
the harness already had.

## The one real error

**The return banner counts unconfirmed first sightings.** With last-read
2026-07-28 it greets: "Since you last read this, on July 28, 2026: **6,423
roles appeared**" — the whole register, because tracking began 07-29 and
`first-seen.json` holds only 145 *confirmed* sightings (its own note: "Only
confirmed roles may be called new", which B's footnote 02 restates and B's NEW
badge obeys). With last-read yesterday it says "7 roles appeared", correctly.
The banner disagrees with the page's own badge rule exactly where the data is
thinnest. A got this right; steal A's clause ("confirmed on two consecutive
nights") verbatim.
