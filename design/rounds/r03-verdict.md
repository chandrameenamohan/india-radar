## B wins a third time, by one point, and that is the finding

All three built the verb, and built it correctly — stated never inferred, dated
by the night it was said, take-backable, burnable. I kept, applied, and
answered in each as a reader across a simulated week, and the second week does
now have a story:

- **B tells it best**, in its native idiom: *"You have applied to 1 of those,
  and nobody has answered yet. Your oldest unanswered application was 6 nights
  ago."* — plus *longest waiting first*, which is the sort a returning person
  actually wants, and a shelf banded *Heard back · Applied to · Kept, and
  nothing said yet* so the week reads forward. The sentence itself conjugated
  ("Show me **the one I applied to** … — what I did last, first"), which is the
  lane question answered with grammar rather than chrome.
- **A owns the round's best sentence**, verified on-page: *"You applied on
  Aug 1, 2026 and have marked no answer since. Whether one came, this page
  cannot know: 3 nights of nothing is not a no."*
- **C owns the round's best mechanism**: the follow-up. *"On Aug 2, 2026 you
  opened Sr. Product Manager · 6Sense. Did you apply?"* — asked once, a night
  later, refusable, dead after 21 days. The only place in nine variants where
  the page uses what it witnessed to help the reader state what it cannot see.

B's margin is the same as always: it is the page a person at 11pm would stay
inside, its ideas compose (the verb landed *inside* the sentence, the week
landed inside the prose, the copy landed inside the letterhead), and its ask is
now the most honest thing the harness has produced — *"What signing in buys,
exactly: nothing yet … pressing it is a vote and nothing else, and we would
rather say so than imply a sync we have not written."* That is both the
scrupulous version and the better pitch, and the builder knew it.

## The round's specific assignments, audited

**The morning after.** All three now keep yesterday and say so. A prints the
full 6,423-line copy under a `KEPT COPY` masthead and stamps the mark into
every hundredth line so depth cannot lie; B serves a byte-for-byte Cache copy
with every tonight-claim verifiably off (0 NEW badges, diff withheld, *Tonight*
re-headed *First seen August 4, 2026* — deixis converted to dates); C keeps the
reader's workbench alive ("What you did is yours, and it does not depend on
this register opening tonight") but not the register — 1,700px of page, no
lines, which its own note 09 overpromises. A's is the most complete answer;
B's the most disciplined; C's the most humane and the thinnest.

**Believed-shipped-but-not-rendered, as predicted.** Found: C's ask prints
*"Your daybook holds 1 nights"* while its NOTES claims that bug fixed; C's
note 09 claims the last sheet prints on a stale morning when only its headline
numbers do. Checked in the other direction: A's fold marks, B's stale
suppressions, and C's departure strikethrough all render exactly as claimed.

**Convergence — it got worse, and it moved underground.** Round 2 converged on
surface prose; round 3 converged on structure. All three now share: the
three-door ask with counted doors · the kept → applied → answered ladder with
per-night dates and take-backs · a nights record · a kept last-good sheet ·
r02-a's diff clause · the honest nothing-yet offer. The voices differ
beautifully — plates, sentences, ledgers — but the mechanism set is one design
wearing three skins, plus one or two variant-private inventions each. When
three independent builders, told only to avoid convergence, converge anyway,
the space around the brief is exhausted. That is what a ceiling looks like
from inside.

## The best of the losers, stated precisely

**From A:**
- *The applied-silence sentence*, verbatim, wherever an application waits: "N
  nights of nothing is not a no. It is the same silence this sheet prints as
  nothing at all."
- *Marking the artifact, not the claims*: on a stale morning the copy's mark
  rides the depth stamps every hundred lines. Suppressing tonight-claims (B)
  prevents lies; marking the whole sheet prevents *mistakes*.
- *The ask that declines to ask*: Clerk dead → no dead button, everything
  prints, `noask` recorded. The gate measuring its own inability to deliver.

**From C:**
- *The two hands*: witnessed events inkless, stated events in the reader's
  colour, "and it never turns one into the other" — with the click handler
  structurally unable to write `applied`.
- *The follow-up*, whole, as specified above. This is the mechanism a real
  product would keep.
- *Printing the overlap*: 119 double-marked postings named, and the bar drawing
  only "the split that does" add to 6,423. The correction of a wound as a
  design element.

## What none of them tried — and the stopping rule

1. **Nobody crossed to the company.** The ladder ends at a posting's `answered`;
   a referral — the product ROLE·ATLAS exists for — is a person vouching for a
   person *into a company*. No variant aggregates "3 applications at 2
   companies", none has a company-shaped view of the reader's week. C's NOTES
   names this and calls its own unit wrong. The next step is not a renderer
   feature; it is the referral loop's data model.
2. **Nobody built anything for the friend.** The judge question "would they
   send it to a friend?" has no answer in nine variants: URL state carries
   filters, but nothing on any page is *made to be handed to somebody* — no
   artifact like round 2's "543 said so themselves" shaped for the telling. The
   loved-website move the harness never briefed, and so never got.
3. **Nobody spends the record.** Three pages now hold nights, keeps,
   applications, answers — and none performs even the honest set operations on
   facts it already holds ("of the 543 within reach, you have opened none").
   Stated-never-inferred forbids guessing; it does not forbid arithmetic. All
   three treat the doctrine as a reason to stop one step earlier than it
   requires.

**The stopping rule, applied.** The winner scored 65.5 against a threshold of
68. The winner's movement across rounds: 63 → 64.5 → 65.5. The mean this round:
63.7 against 62.5 — and the floor did not rise this time; the wildcard fell
back on harder ground. Closing named gaps remains the one thing this harness
does reliably, the unbriefed surplus per round is shrinking (this round: one
sentence, one grammar, one question), and structural convergence says the
variants are now finding the same walls. **The recommendation is to stop, and
per the brief that recommendation is accepted by writing it: this page's
renderer has found its ceiling at a very good register, and the remaining
distance to "loved" is not in the renderer.**

## The highest-value next steps, outside the renderer

In order:

1. **The Worker's first real endpoint.** All three asks now say, in nearly the
   same words, that signing in buys *nothing yet* — three independent designs
   converged on naming the same absent server. Build `PUT /keeps` on the Clerk
   session (A's `ponytail:` names it; C's ask names the offer: "a daybook
   should outlive a browser"). The gate's sign-in counts only become
   decision-grade once yes buys the thing every variant already promises.
2. **The referral loop's unit.** Move the model from posting to company +
   person: registered partners who can vouch, and a reader-stated "I applied"
   feeding a company-scoped view. Gap 1 above is the design brief for it, and
   C's follow-up is the interaction to keep.
3. **The bilingual occupation vocabulary, in the build.** Third round running
   that every variant hand-rolls a 12–24-word lexicon and says on-page that the
   real fix is upstream. The renderers have been honest about this ceiling
   three times; the build should stop making them be.
