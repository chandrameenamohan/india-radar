# Round 3 — the brief, and the last one unless it clears 68

Read `design/rounds/r02-verdict.md` before this. `design/BRIEF.md` still stands.

**The bar is 64.5** (r02-b, judge's 70). **The stopping rule is 68.** If this
round's winner does not clear it, the harness has found this page's ceiling and
the remaining distance belongs to the product — the referral loop, the Worker,
the build's vocabulary — not to another lap of the renderer. That is the judge's
recommendation and it is accepted.

## Do not re-brief round 2's gaps

Reads the reader, the return visit, keeping something, the cursor following the
eye — **all four are closed in all three variants, verified as a reader.** Round
2's mean rose 57.5 → 62.5 while the winner moved only 63 → 64.5: the floor rose
to meet the old ceiling. Closing named gaps is what this harness now does
reliably. Re-polishing them produces decimal points.

It also converged all three variants on the same honesty mechanics — stated
profiles, printed counters, bookplates, refusal prose — to the point where whole
paragraphs are interchangeable between them. **Convergence is the warning sign.**
A mechanism that appears in all three in identical form is no longer an idea; it
is a convention you inherited, and inheriting it without deciding to is exactly
what cost r01-a its originality points.

## The three things nobody has built

### 1. The register knows what the reader did about it

Every variant ends at *Open the posting ↗*. No reader can state **applied**.

So the keeps are a shelf, never a workbench, and the second week has no story:
a shortlist that cannot change state teaches nothing when you return to it. This
is one doctrine-clean bit in `localStorage` — a reader-stated fact, exactly like
the profile, stated never inferred, burnable like everything else.

**It is also the whole product.** ROLE·ATLAS exists to get a person referred into
a company, and that lives exactly one step past this page's last control. The
loved-website move past collecting is *progress*: kept → applied → answered.

Build it so a reader can see their own week.

### 2. Design the morning after the build moves

Mid-round-2 the nightly rewrote the corpus — schema 10 → 11, 371 → 789
companies, 6,423 → 27,687 roles — and **all three variants went dark together.**
Each refused honestly, which is correct: a renderer must not guess at a contract
it does not hold. r02-b did it best; its headline numbers became em-dashes.

**The uniformity is the tell.** It is inherited convention, and none of the three
designed what comes next. The keeps survive in `localStorage`; the register they
point into does not. So the 11pm reader who comes back after a schema bump meets
an apology instead of yesterday's copy, marked *yesterday's*.

A page that diffs itself nightly must expect to be stale nightly. Keep the last
good sheet and say what it is. Absence stays absence — a stale sheet must never
present itself as current — but a dated copy is not a lie, and an apology with no
register is not honesty, it is just an empty page.

### 3. Visit seven

All three greet the *second* visit with one diff banner. None accretes. r02-a
counts `ra2.visits` and does nothing with the number.

No nights-read strip, no history for the reader's own keeps, no sense that the
register and the reader have become old acquaintances. **Habit is built from
accretion**, and the material — visit dates, keep dates, diff sizes — is already
in `localStorage` in all three. Nobody spent it.

## Steal these whole — they are round 2's best, and two are from the losers

- **r02-a's overprint rule as a system.** One colour, laid only where a fact
  exists: the reader's ticks, folds, cursor, focus, and a registration cross that
  appears only once a second plate exists. The mostly-bare sheet under a stated
  standpoint presented as *the finding* rather than as an empty result.
- **r02-a's three-door ask.** SIGN IN · **PRINT THE REST ANYWAY** · NO THANK YOU,
  each counted, `printed anyway` its own ledger line. Bypass as a *labelled door*
  is the strongest trust gesture either round produced.
- **r02-a's fold-as-departure-ledger.** The build records no departures — but a
  folded URL that has left the corpus prints struck through. The one place this
  register can truthfully say *gone*.
- **r02-a's diff clause, verbatim, wherever a diff is shown.** *"145 … confirmed
  on two consecutive nights. What vanished, this copy cannot tell you … a count
  it does not hold is a count this page will not invent."* r02-b's greeting
  claims "6,423 roles appeared" when only 145 are confirmed, and r02-c prints the
  same overclaim as a bare `+6,423` chip. **Only r02-a got this right.**
- **r02-c's distance, derived and marked `≈`, with nearest-first ordering.**
  State where you can work and the register becomes a staircase. The best
  wordless answer to *what could I do* in either round.
- **r02-c's `designer → 107`.** Translate the reader's word *toward* the boards,
  never a board's word toward the reader.
- **r02-b's shortlist as a word in its own sentence**, if you are in that lane.

## Two shared tells worth naming

- **All three hand-roll a private ja→en lexicon** of 12–24 concepts for the
  language bridge. The real fix is a bilingual occupation vocabulary in the
  *build*, which no renderer can reach. Do not pretend otherwise — a hand lexicon
  that says what it is beats one that implies coverage it lacks.
- **All three offer the same thing for signing in** — an account carries your
  keeps across devices — and **the current Worker cannot honour any of it.**
  r02-a says so, r02-b says *could*, r02-c says *would*. Only one of those three
  is true today.
